I am a Ph.D. student in Mechanical and Aerospace Engineering at George Washington University, advised by Prof. Peng Wei. I work at the intersection of air traffic control and machine learning.

I received both my B.S. and M.S. in Air Transportation from Korea Aerospace University. As an undergraduate, I studied air traffic control and earned an air traffic controller license. During my master's studies, advised by Prof. Keumjin Lee, I worked on multi-agent trajectory modeling, generative models for flight trajectories, and automatic speech recognition for air traffic control.

My research focuses on developing reliable and safe autonomous systems capable of high-level reasoning. I am particularly interested in generative models for prediction and planning under uncertainty, as well as methods for structured and reliable decision making. I believe machines can eventually perform complex tasks traditionally carried out by humans, such as air traffic control and driving, and ultimately perform them even better.

Research Keywords: air traffic management, trajectory prediction, generative models, multi-agent systems, decision making under uncertainty

Research

I work on machine learning models for air traffic, with an emphasis on making their predictions both accurate and interpretable to the people who have to act on them. Representative papers are highlighted below.

Multi-Agent Inverted Transformer for Flight Trajectory Prediction
Seokbin Yoon, Keumjin Lee
IEEE Transactions on Intelligent Transportation Systems, 2026
paper / doi

MAIFormer predicts the trajectories of many aircraft at once using two attention modules: masked multivariate attention for each aircraft's spatio-temporal behavior, and agent attention for the social interactions between flights. Its attention maps are readable by humans, which makes the predictions usable in an air traffic control setting.

Airport Terminal Passenger Queue Forecasting for Departure Gates and Security Checkpoints
Juhwan Lee, Seokbin Yoon, Keumjin Lee, Hojong Baik, Seyeon Jung
AIAA DATC/IEEE Digital Avionics Systems Conference (DASC), 2026
Best Paper of Session Award
arXiv

A Transformer-based forecaster that learns temporal dependencies and inter-facility correlations across departure gates, security checkpoints and check-in islands, giving accurate queue length and waiting time predictions up to two hours ahead.

Probabilistic Multi-Agent Aircraft Landing Time Prediction
Kyungmin Kim, Seokbin Yoon, Keumjin Lee
AIAA SciTech, 2026
Best Student Paper Award in Intelligent Systems
paper / doi

Landing times are modeled as distributions rather than point estimates, so the framework reports the uncertainty alongside the prediction. Attention scores expose the radar vectoring patterns controllers use, adding explainability.

Learning to Explain Air Traffic Situation
Hong-ah Chai, Seokbin Yoon, Keumjin Lee
US-Europe Air Transportation Research & Development Symposium, 2025
arXiv

A Transformer-based multi-agent trajectory model whose attention scores quantify how much each aircraft influences the overall traffic situation, offering a window into how controllers build their mental picture of the airspace.

Aircraft Trajectory Dataset Augmentation in Latent Space
Seokbin Yoon, Keumjin Lee
International Journal of Aeronautical and Space Sciences, 2025
paper

ATRADA encodes trajectories with a Transformer, reduces them with PCA, fits a Gaussian mixture in the reduced space, and decodes new samples back into full trajectories — producing synthetic flight data that keeps the structure of the original dataset.

Aircraft Trajectory Prediction with Inverted Transformer
Seokbin Yoon, Keumjin Lee
IEEE Access, 2025
paper / doi

Instead of embedding one token per time step, each series is embedded independently as a variate token. Capturing multivariate correlations this way improves prediction accuracy over standard deep learning baselines on Incheon terminal airspace data.
